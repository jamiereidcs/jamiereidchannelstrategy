Most of Your Partners Aren't Failing. They're Waiting.

Every program has a dormant majority, and most of them get a newsletter instead of a strategy. Sort your quiet partners into waiting, stalled, and gone, then re-engage, cut, and recruit with intent.

Jamie Reid September 2026 5 min read

Every partner program has a number nobody likes to say out loud. In my recent poll, most respondents said fewer than 40% of their partners are actively selling. That means the majority of the roster you recruited, onboarded, and enabled is doing nothing.

Ask most channel leaders what they do about that, and the honest answer is some version of "not much." Dormant partners don't get a strategy. They get a newsletter.

I think we've been treating dormancy as a failure when it's really a design problem. Some of those partners are waiting for a reason to sell. Some are stalled on something you can fix. And some are gone, and you're paying to pretend they aren't.

More touchpoints make it worse

The default response to a quiet partner is more outreach. More check-ins, more webinars, more emails. My earlier poll on partner relationship fatigue came back with "all of the above": too many touchpoints, not enough meaningful engagement, unrealistic expectations, and poor follow-up, all at once.

That's the trap. We chase silence with volume, partners tune us out further, and then we wonder why opt-in rates are low. A partner who hasn't registered a deal in two quarters doesn't need a fifth reminder about your certification path. They need a reason.

Waiting, stalled, or gone

Before you do anything, sort your dormant partners into three groups.

Group 1

Waiting

They like your product and have no current trigger. No customer is asking, no deal needs you, no renewal is coming up. These partners aren't broken. They're between moments.

Group 2

Stalled

Something is in the way. The champion left. They never finished enablement. They registered a deal once and had a bad experience with channel conflict. These are fixable, and they're usually worth fixing.

Group 3

Gone

Their business moved away from your category, they signed with a competitor, or they joined for the logo and never intended to sell. No campaign will change that.

The diagnosis doesn't need to be complicated. Look at last deal registration, last portal login, enablement completion, and whether your champion is still there. One honest conversation with the partner will usually confirm the rest.

If you already run a partner performance scorecard, the engagement and activity layer is your early warning system here. A partner rarely goes from active to gone overnight. The signals show up there first.

How to re-engage the ones worth keeping

For waiting partners, stop manufacturing activity. Move them to a low-touch tier with quarterly relevance instead of weekly check-ins, and build trigger-based outreach instead. When account mapping shows a shared customer, when a customer in their base hits a renewal window, or when you launch something that solves a problem their customers have, that's when you reach out. You want to be the obvious answer when their moment arrives.

For stalled partners, fix the specific blocker. If the champion left, find the new one and re-onboard them personally. If enablement never stuck, skip the full curriculum and walk them through one deal. If they got burned on channel conflict, own it and show them what changed.

One more thing from my polls: executive dinners and small groups beat trade shows for lead quality. The same logic applies here. A handful of your stalled partners deserve a real conversation with a leader, not another automated sequence.

How to end it cleanly

Terminating a partner feels like admitting a mistake, so most programs never do it. But a roster full of inactive partners costs you. It inflates your numbers, dilutes your team's attention, and creates conflict risk when a dormant partner suddenly shows up on a deal they did nothing to earn.

Put activity requirements in your program terms from the start, so termination is a policy and not a personal decision. When a partner hits the threshold, tell them directly, give them a defined window to re-engage, and offer a clear path back if their business changes later. Handled well, termination is respectful. It tells the partner you take the relationship seriously enough to be honest about it.

Recruit for the partners you actually need

Once you've cleaned up the roster, the real question is what you replace it with. This is where AI earns its place, and not as a headline. As a research assistant.

Start with your ideal partner profile. My poll respondents said the two factors that matter most are technical capability and cultural alignment. Both are hard to see from firmographics alone, and both leave signals AI can find faster than your team can.

Use AI to scan for signals that predict selling, not just fit. Is the partner hiring for skills tied to your category? Does their services page describe the problem you solve? Do they already work with the tools you integrate with? Do their customers overlap with yours in your account mapping data? Then have it summarize each prospect against your profile so your team spends time on conversations, not research.

Also point it back at your own data. Look at the partners who became active fastest and have AI find what they had in common. Your best predictor of a good future partner is usually hiding in your existing roster.

The real goal is a smaller roster that sells

Partner investment is already heading toward fewer, deeper partners. The programs that win won't have the biggest partner count. They'll have the clearest picture of who is waiting, who is stalled, and who is gone, and the discipline to act on it.
